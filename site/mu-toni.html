Generative Arpeggiator Mono-Synth

μ-toniPick a chord. Let it play.

Choose a scale and a chord, and μ-toni plays it one note at a time, climbing and falling through as many octaves as you like. Each note is played by a two-oscillator wavetable synth with its own envelopes. It works out every note as it goes, so you can move any control while it plays.

What it does

An arpeggiator that thinks
for itself.

A normal arpeggiator plays back a list of notes. μ-toni stores no list. It works out each note from the scale, chord, root, octaves and direction as they are right now, so changing any control reshapes the arpeggio on the very next step.

Scale and chord pool

Twelve scales and about thirty-five chords, from simple triads to altered jazz shapes. Inversion re-voices the chord, and you can stack it over one to four octaves. Snap keeps every note strictly in key.

Appergater

Skewed-triangle scan

Direction runs from always falling, through up-and-down, to always climbing. The shorter sweep skips notes evenly so the timing stays steady. The pattern is the same on every pass. Nothing is random.

Direction

Wavetable mono synth

Two wavetable oscillators plus noise, with FM, phase and ring modulation between them. Sixteen filter types, and amp, filter and pitch envelopes. Legato and glide give you 303-style slides.

Sound

Modulate almost everything

Eight modulators per layer, smooth or stepped. A stepped modulator on Chord makes a chord progression. One on Root walks the key. A slow one on Direction makes the arpeggio breathe.

Modulation

Up to eight layers

Each layer is its own arpeggiator and synth. Stack them for layered, detuned or interlocking arpeggios over one shared tempo. A new μ-toni starts with one layer.

Layers

Play it from a keyboard

Hold a key and it becomes the root of the arpeggio. Set the trigger to MIDI and the pattern plays only while you hold a key. The same mixer, effects and presets as the rest of the μ-series.

MIDI & mixer
μ-toni interface

Chords in. Arpeggios out.

Sound sections on top, the Appergater in the middle and eight modulators underneath. Every control in the Appergater can be modulated.

Sound

Two wavetable oscillators, X-Mod, filter, three envelopes and a per-layer insert effect.

The Appergater

Scale, chord, rate, root, inversion, octaves, direction, gate, glide and an accent pattern.

Mixer

The shared μ-series mixer: sidechain, three sends and two master inserts.

Where the name comes from

The name comes from a customer of a music shop where I used to work in Bristol. His name was Tony. No matter how many times he was told that an arpeggiator was called an arpeggiator, an Italian word, Tony always called them “appagators”.

The Settings page carries his often asked question:

Has it got an appagator, is it a powerful tool?

Bless him, he was a bit special. I wonder what became of him.

System requirements

System

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. macOS is on hold, and Linux is in beta (VST3, CLAP and Standalone).

Formats

VST3 and CLAP plug-ins for your DAW, plus a Standalone app that runs on its own.

You will need

A DAW that hosts VST3 or CLAP plug-ins (for the plug-in), or just an audio interface or sound card (for the Standalone).
